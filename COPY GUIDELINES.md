# Copywriting Guidelines

Every piece of text a parent sees — buttons, card titles, notifications,
empty states, error messages — should follow these rules.

## Tone
Warm, plain, direct. Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a clinical report
and not a hype-y app. Short sentences. No jargon without explanation.

## Never use — and why

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Your child is behind" | Implies failure | "This skill may benefit from extra practice" |
| "Poor performance" | Judgmental | "Here's one small step that could help" |
| "Failure" / "Delay detected" | Clinical, alarming | "Every child progresses at their own pace" |
| "AI predicts..." | Implies false certainty | "Based on your data so far..." |
| "X% confidence" | Fabricated-sounding statistic | "🟡 6 days of data logged" |
| "You missed your streak" | Guilt-based | "Even one small activity today counts" |

## Empty states are encouragement, not dead ends
Bad: "No data available."
Good: "We don't have enough information to show trends yet — logging
activities for a week will unlock this."

## Every claim needs a source a parent could check
If a sentence states a fact ("You've completed 5 of 7 days"), it must be
something the parent could verify by looking at their own logged history.
Never write a specific-sounding number that isn't a real query result.

## Affirmations are evidence-based, not generic
Bad: "Great job today!"
Good: "You've logged activities 5 days in a row — your longest streak yet."

## Never write in Level 3 (clinical) language
No "regression," "developmental concern," "screening indicates," or anything
implying a clinical read of the child's condition. That tier is not yet built
(see AI_GUARDRAILS.md) and copy should never get ahead of what the product
actually does.

## Disclaimers
Keep the medical disclaimer visible but not alarming — reassuring in tone,
clear in content: "This is educational guidance, not a medical diagnosis.
Always consult a qualified professional for your child's health."
