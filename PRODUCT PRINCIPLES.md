# Product Principles

Companion document to VISION.md and NEUROSARATHI_TRUST_CHARTER.md — the
practical, day-to-day product decisions that follow from them.

## 1. Truth over impressiveness
If we don't have enough data, clearly say so. Never estimate silently.

## 2. Evidence before intelligence
Every insight should be traceable to actual user data, parent logs,
assessments, or clinician input — never simulated analytics.

## 3. AI should explain, not invent
Recommendations must be based on observable patterns in real data, not
plausible-sounding guesses.

## 4. Progress over prediction
In early versions, prioritize helping parents understand what has already
happened and what to do next, over forecasting uncertain outcomes.

## 5. Trust is the competitive advantage
Parents must feel confident every number and every recommendation has a
genuine basis. This matters more than looking sophisticated.

## 6. Progressive Intelligence
The product gets smarter only as real data accumulates — never jump ahead of
the evidence:

Level 1: General educational guidance
→ Level 2: Personalized reminders
→ Level 3: Pattern detection (grounded, not statistical)
→ Level 4: Explainable, evidence-backed recommendations
→ Level 5: Long-term developmental insight (requires real longitudinal data
and clinical review — not yet built)

## 7. Parent trust before engagement
Never introduce a feature solely to increase usage. Every notification must
answer "does this genuinely help the family today?" If not, don't send it.

## 8. Graceful handling of missing data
The dashboard should never appear broken or empty. Instead, it guides the
parent toward the next meaningful step: "We don't have enough information to
identify trends yet. Logging activities for a week will unlock personalized
insight." An empty state is an invitation, not a dead end.

## 9. Every screen must help
Every page should answer at least one of: What should I do today? Is my child
improving? Why is this happening? What comes next? Where can I get help? Am I
alone? If a screen answers none of these, it should be redesigned.

## 10. Human-centered language, always
Never use language that blames the parent or the child. Avoid: "your child is
behind," "poor performance," "failure," "delay detected." Use instead: "this
skill may benefit from extra practice," "here's one small step that could
help today," "every child progresses at their own pace." See
COPY_GUIDELINES.md for the full reference.
